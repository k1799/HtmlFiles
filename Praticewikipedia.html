Tom Holland

This article is about the British actor. For other people named Tom or Thomas Holland, see Tom Holland (disambiguation) and Thomas Holland. Not to be confused with Tom Hollander.

Thomas Stanley Holland (born 1 June 1996) is a British actor known for his work in a diverse range of genres. His accolades include a BAFTA Award as well as two Critics' Choice Awards nominations. Holland's films as a leading actor have grossed over $13.1 billion worldwide, making him one of the highest-grossing actors of all time. He was featured on the Forbes 30 Under 30 Europe list of 2019.

Holland's career began at age nine when he enrolled in a dancing class, where a choreographer noticed him and arranged for him to audition for a role in Billy Elliot the Musical at London's Victoria Palace Theatre. After two years of training, he secured a supporting part in 2008 and was upgraded to the title role that year, which he played until 2010. Holland made his film debut in the disaster drama The Impossible (2012) as a teenage tourist trapped in a tsunami, for which he received a London Film Critics Circle Award for Young British Performer of the Year. After this, Holland decided to pursue acting as a full-time career, appearing in How I Live Now (2013), In the Heart of the Sea (2015) and The Lost City of Z (2016).

Early Life

Thomas Stanley Holland was born on 1 June 1996 in Kingston upon Thames, in south-west London, to photographer Nicola (née Frost) and comedian-author Dominic Holland.[1] He has three younger brothers.[2] His paternal grandmother was Irish from County Tipperary, Ireland and his paternal grandfather was Manx from the Isle of Man.[3][4][5] He lives in Kingston upon Thames, near the house of his parents and younger brothers.[6] As his parents have creative professions, he is often inspired by them;[7] he considers his father a role model who has unofficially worked as his manager due to his experience in the industry.[8][9]

Holland was educated at Donhead Preparatory School, an all-male Catholic preparatory school in Wimbledon, in the Merton borough of south west London.[10] When he was seven, he was diagnosed with dyslexia. His parents sent him and his brothers (to avoid making them feel neglected) to a private school so he could get the necessary attention. Although Holland liked the new school, this started to drain his family's finances.[11][12] Holland attended Wimbledon College, a voluntary aided Jesuit comprehensive school,[13] followed by the BRIT School for Performing Arts and Technology in the Selhurst area of Croydon.[14]
